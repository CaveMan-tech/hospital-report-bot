## Executive summary (10 lines)

1. SOLID: Constitution Art 43(2) ("A person shall not be denied emergency medical treatment") and Health Act 2017 s.7 (right to emergency treatment; offence for a "medical institution"; fine "not exceeding three million shillings") were read in primary text. They apply to public and private providers. Neither says emergency care is FREE.
2. SOLID: A long, consistent line of High Court decisions (2015 to Aug 2026) holds that detaining a patient, or a body, over an unpaid bill is unlawful/unconstitutional (Arts 28, 29, 39; "no lien", "no property in a dead body"). The leading public-hospital case is the Pumwani Maternity case, [2015] KEHC 8096 (KLR). All read on Kenya Law.
3. IMPORTANT NUANCE: those same courts say the bill remains a debt; release is often ordered on a written undertaking, security, or payment of mortuary charges only; one 2025 case against a public national hospital (KUTRRH) called detention "wrong" but dismissed the petition with no damages. There is still NO statute expressly criminalising detention; two Health (Amendment) Bills were pending at last check (Feb to Apr 2026). Bot text must not promise an outcome.
4. SOLID: Kenya National Patients' Rights Charter (Ministry of Health, October 2013) wording for emergency treatment, dignity, informed consent, information and complaint was read from the official booklet. No revised edition found. Health Act ss.5(2), 8, 9, 11, 13, 14 read in primary text.
5. SOLID: Obstetric-violence precedent: JOO (Josephine Majani) v Attorney General [2018] KEHC 7540 (KLR), upheld by the Court of Appeal [2024] KECA 1377 (KLR). Public county hospital, KSh 2.5m damages, formal apology ordered.
6. NEW AND SHAKY: a national emergency medical number, 922 ("SHA 922 Lifeline"), was launched on 5 Aug 2026 with a government promise that ambulance transport and the first 24 hours of emergency treatment are paid from the Emergency, Chronic and Critical Illness Fund regardless of SHA registration. This is six weeks old, reported by news media only, and its real-world reliability is unknown. SHA also issued a no-deposit directive to contracted hospitals on 1 Aug 2026.
7. SHAKY: reliability of 999/112 (widely described as unreliable for ambulances), Nairobi County's 1508 line (seen only in a search snippet of the county site), Befrienders Kenya hours, FIDA toll-free line (seen on FIDA Facebook/annual report, not the contact page), Nursing Council complaints email (two domains in circulation).
8. COULD NOT VERIFY: any actual prosecution under Health Act s.7(3); whether ECCIF regulations are gazetted and paying claims reliably; current status of the two Health (Amendment) Bills after April 2026; names of current Nairobi County health office-holders; KMHFR check of the fictional hospital names (registry was unreachable).
9. CAUTIONARY: a May 2026 High Court judgment (Longisa County Referral Hospital, [2026] KEHC 5723 (KLR)) dismissed an emergency-refusal/deposit claim for lack of evidence. This supports the bot's "note time, department and who you spoke to" step, and warns against telling users they "will win".
10. Evidence for the pitch is good for detention (MoH figures to Parliament: 300 patients and 391 bodies held, 2018) and for childbirth abuse (Abuya et al. 2015: 20% any disrespect/abuse, 8.1% detained) and staff absence (World Bank SDI 2018: 56.7% absence in public facilities, mostly approved absence). It is weakest for a hard statistic on emergency refusal: there are strong cases and official directives but no prevalence figure found.

## A. Emergency treatment law

### A1. Constitution of Kenya 2010, Article 43

Exact text (HIGH, read on Kenya Law, https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2010/constitution):

> "43. Economic and social rights (1) Every person has the right— (a) to the highest attainable standard of health, which includes the right to health care services, including reproductive health care; ... (2) A person shall not be denied emergency medical treatment."

Notes:
- "A person", not "a citizen": applies to everyone in Kenya. HIGH.
- Bill of Rights binds "all State organs and all persons" (Art 20(1)), which is why courts have applied it to private hospitals too. MEDIUM (Art 20 not re-read today; stated from general knowledge and reflected in the private-hospital judgments below).
- Art 43(2) says treatment cannot be DENIED. It does not say it is free.

### A2. Health Act, No. 21 of 2017

Source: Laws of Kenya, Health Act No. 21 of 2017, Revised Edition 2022, published by the National Council for Law Reporting. Kenya Law page: https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2017/21/eng@2022-12-31 . Text read from the official PDF mirror https://machakosassembly.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Health-Act-21-of-2017.pdf . Original PDF on Kenya Law: https://kenyalaw.org/kl/fileadmin/pdfdownloads/Acts/HealthActNo.21of2017.pdf . Confidence HIGH for the wording as at the 2022 revision. UNVERIFIED whether any amendment after 31 Dec 2022 touched ss.2, 7, 12 or 14 (the pending 2024/2025 amendment bills would; see B4).

Definitions, s.2 (HIGH):
> "'emergency treatment' refers to necessary immediate health care that must be administered to prevent death or worsening of a medical situation;"

> "'medical emergency' means an acute situation of injury or illness that poses an immediate risk to life or health of a person or has potential for deterioration in the health of a person or if not managed timely would lead to adverse consequences in the well-being;"

Section 7, in full (HIGH):
> "7. Emergency treatment (1) Every person has the right to emergency medical treatment. (2) For the purposes of this section, emergency medical treatment shall include— (a) pre-hospital care; (b) stabilizing the health status of the individual; or (c) arranging for referral in cases where the health provider of first call does not have facilities or capability to stabilize the health status of the victim. (3) Any medical institution that fails to provide emergency medical treatment while having ability to do so commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to a fine not exceeding three million shillings."

Section 12(2) (HIGH):
> "All healthcare providers, whether in the public or private sector, shall have the duty— ... (b) to provide emergency medical treatment as provided for under section 7(2);"

Section 15(1)(x) (HIGH): the national Ministry shall "establish an emergency medical treatment fund for emergencies to provide for unforeseen situations calling for supplementary finance".

Precision points for bot text:
- Penalty: a fine "not exceeding three million shillings" (KSh 3,000,000), on conviction, against the "medical institution". It is a maximum, not a fixed amount. There is no imprisonment term in s.7. HIGH.
- Qualifier: "while having ability to do so". A facility without the capability must still arrange referral under s.7(2)(c). HIGH.
- Public and private: yes, both (s.12(2) wording; s.7 says "any medical institution"). HIGH.
- FREE? The Act does NOT say emergency treatment is free and does not expressly mention deposits. It says treatment cannot be withheld. Do not write "emergency treatment is free by law". HIGH (absence confirmed by reading ss.2 to 15).
- The Patients' Rights Charter (C1 below) is the instrument that uses the words "irrespective of the patient's ability to pay". It is a Ministry policy document, not a statute.
- Prosecutions under s.7(3): I found none reported. UNVERIFIED either way.

### A3. Social Health Insurance Act, No. 16 of 2023, and the ECCIF

Source: Kenya Law, https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2023/16/eng@2023-11-24 (HIGH).

- s.2 repeats the Health Act definition of "emergency treatment" almost word for word.
- s.28: "There is established a Fund be known as the Emergency, Chronic and Critical Illness Fund to— (a) defray the costs of management of chronic illnesses after depletion of the social health insurance cover; and (b) to cover the costs of emergency treatment."
- s.29: funded by money appropriated by the National Assembly, gifts/grants, other lawful sources (so it depends on budget allocations, not member contributions).
- s.30: the Cabinet Secretary makes regulations to implement it.
- Naming: the Act says "Emergency, Chronic and Critical Illness Fund" (ECCIF). Media and SHA sometimes write "Emergency, Critical and Chronic Illness Fund". Use the Act's wording.

Is it operational? Evidence as of Sept 2026 (all MEDIUM, news reports; no primary SHA document opened):
- SHA notice dated 1 Aug 2026 signed by SHA CEO Dr Mercy Mwangangi to contracted providers, as quoted by Kenyans.co.ke: "No patient requiring emergency medical treatment shall be denied, delayed, referred or transferred solely because of inability to pay, lack of benefit confirmation, or failure to provide a deposit, advance payment or financial guarantee". Eligible emergency treatment in the first 24 hours is to be financed through ECCIF; afterwards SHIF or "other applicable payment arrangements" apply. Sanction: de-empanelment / contract termination. Sources: https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/125800-sha-directs-hospitals-not-deny-emergency-care-over-payment ; https://capitalfm.africa/sha-warns-hospitals-against-turning-away-patients-seeking-emergency-care/ ; https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/health/health-science/article/2001554436/sha-warns-hospitals-against-denying-patients-emergency-care . Note Capital FM paraphrases the quote slightly differently; get the original SHA circular before quoting it.
- 5 Aug 2026: President Ruto launched the "SHA 922 Lifeline" and National Ambulance Dispatch Centre. Reported promise: ambulance transport plus first 24 hours of emergency treatment free for all Kenyans whether SHA-registered or not, funded from ECCIF; more than 600 accredited ambulances (county, Kenya Red Cross, St John) linked. Sources: https://citizen.digital/article/govt-launches-922-emergency-response-system-with-free-ambulance-services-n387792 ; https://allafrica.com/stories/202608060070.html ; https://capitalfm.africa/ruto-launches-the-sha-922-lifeline-mobile-service-and-national-ambulance-centre/ .
- Reasons for caution: SHA owes providers large unpaid claims (a mid-2026 report put private hospitals' claims at over KSh 76 billion; MEDIUM, search summary of Nation/Star coverage), which is the incentive behind deposit demands. Health CS Aden Duale had already had to warn hospitals on 10 Mar 2026 not to turn patients away (Capital FM, above). The fact that SHA needed to issue the 1 Aug 2026 directive is itself evidence that deposit demands for emergencies continue.
- Bottom line for bot text: it is safe to say the LAW forbids refusing emergency treatment. It is NOT yet safe to tell a frightened person "the government will pay" or "it is free". At most: "The government has said the Social Health Authority pays for the first 24 hours of emergency treatment" and only after a Kenyan practitioner confirms how this works on the ground.

## B. Detention of patients and bodies over unpaid bills

### B1. Legal basis in one paragraph

There is no Kenyan statute that lets a hospital hold a person or a body as security for a bill, and (as of the last status found) no statute that expressly criminalises doing so. The law comes from the Constitution as applied by the High Court: Art 28 (dignity: "Every person has inherent dignity and the right to have that dignity respected and protected"), Art 29 (freedom and security of the person, including the right not to be "(a) deprived of freedom arbitrarily or without just cause" and not "(f) treated or punished in a cruel, inhuman or degrading manner"), Art 39(1) ("Every person has the right to freedom of movement"), plus Art 24 (rights may be limited only by law), Art 43, Art 45, Art 53 in particular cases, and ICCPR Arts 9 and 12. For bodies: "no property in a dead body", therefore no lien, plus dignity (Art 28) and the Public Health Act (Cap 242) on disposal of bodies. All article texts HIGH (read on Kenya Law).

High Court decisions bind magistrates' courts and are persuasive (not strictly binding) on other High Court judges. No Court of Appeal or Supreme Court decision squarely on hospital detention was found. So media phrases like "binding precedent" and "High Court bans" overstate things slightly. MEDIUM (general legal principle; confirm with counsel).

## C. Patients' rights instruments

### C1. The Kenya National Patients' Rights Charter, 2013

- Issuer: Ministry of Health, Republic of Kenya. First edition. Acknowledgment signed by Prof. Fred H. K. Segor, Principal Secretary, "October, 2013". Drafting secretariat led by the CEO of the then Medical Practitioners and Dentists Board. HIGH (read from the scanned booklet).
- Official PDF (hosted by the regulator): https://kmpdc.go.ke/resources/PATIENTS_CHARTER_2013.pdf . A copy is also hosted by Amnesty Kenya: https://www.amnestykenya.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/PATIENTS-CHARTER-BOOKLET-FINAL_compressed.pdf (not opened). A health.go.ke URL was not found.
- Status: still the current charter as far as I can find; no revised edition located. It is a policy document, not legislation, but Health Act s.10(f) refers to users' rights "as provided for in the applicable service charters". MEDIUM for "still current".

Exact wording (HIGH; Chapter 1, "Every person, patient or client, has a:-"):
- Right 2: "Right to receive emergency treatment in any health facility. In emergency situations, irrespective of the patient's ability to pay, treatment to stabilize the patient's condition shall be provided."
- Right 5: "Right to the highest attainable quality of Health care products and services."
- Right 8: "Right to informed consent to treatment. To be given full and accurate information in a language one understands about the nature of one's illness, diagnostic procedures, proposed treatment, alternative treatment and the costs involved for one to make a decision except in emergency cases. The decision shall be made willingly and free from duress."
- Right 9: "Right to information. Every patient is entitled to receiving full and accurate information concerning their health and health care."
- Right 10: "Right to be treated with respect and dignity." (heading only; no further text)
- Right 12: "Right to complain. Every person has a right to complain about health services to the relevant authorities, such complaint should be investigated and receive a response from the authority within a reasonable time that does not exceed twelve months. Where there is a delay, the relevant authority shall provide the reasons."
- Chapter 2, responsibility 10: "To enquire about costs of treatment and rehabilitation and to make appropriate arrangements for payments"; responsibility 15: "To express any concerns through the right channels confidentially."
- Chapter 3 (dispute resolution): complain (1) directly to the provider, (2) to the relevant regulatory body, (3) in court.

### C2. Health Act 2017: rights, duties, complaints (HIGH, same source as A2)

- s.5(2): "Every person shall have the right to be treated with dignity, respect and have their privacy respected in accordance with the Constitution and this Act."
- s.5(3): national and county governments "shall ensure the provision of free and compulsory— (a) vaccination for children under five years of age; and (b) maternity care." (Relevant to childbirth cases in public facilities.)
- s.8: duty to inform the user of health status, options, "benefits, risks, costs and consequences", and the right to refuse, "in a language that the user understands".
- s.9: no health service without informed consent, with listed exceptions including "(d) the patient is being treated in an emergency situation".
- s.11: confidentiality.
- s.12(1)(c): a provider may refuse to treat a user who is "physically or verbally abusive ... except in an emergency situation where no alternative health care personnel is available". (Practical: advise users to stay calm.)
- s.13: duties of users, including "(e) to treat healthcare providers and health workers with dignity and respect".
- s.14(1): "Any person has a right to file a complaint about the manner in which he or she was treated at a health facility and have the complaint investigated appropriately."
- s.14(3): complaint procedures must "(a) be displayed by all health facilities in a manner that is visible for any person entering the establishment" and "(b) be primarily handled by the head of the relevant facility or any person designated by the facility".
- s.14(4): the complainant "has a right to be informed, in writing and within a period of three months from the date the complaint was lodged, of the action taken or decision made".
- s.14(5): if the facility or a regulatory body fails to resolve it, "the Authority shall take necessary action" (the Authority is the Kenya Health Professions Oversight Authority, KHPOA).
- Tension to flag: Health Act says 3 months; the 2013 Charter says up to 12 months. The Act is later and is law; prefer 3 months.

### C3. Obstetric violence / disrespectful maternity care

## F. How public hospitals are organised and who is accountable

Devolution. Under the Fourth Schedule of the Constitution, county governments run "county health facilities"; the national government keeps health policy and "national referral health facilities". MEDIUM (from general knowledge; Schedule not re-read today). Confirmed in practice by the Pumwani judgment, which made "the county government of Nairobi, which has the responsibility of management and administration of" Pumwani pay the damages (HIGH).

Levels (Health Act 2017, First Schedule; HIGH): Level 1 community health services; Level 2 dispensary/clinic; Level 3 health centre; Level 4 primary hospital; Level 5 secondary hospital; Level 6 tertiary hospital ("Level 6 shall be National Referral Hospitals"). Everyday usage: Level 4 is roughly a "sub-county hospital" (formerly "district hospital"), Level 5 a "county referral hospital", Level 6 a "national referral hospital" (MEDIUM, usage).

Nairobi public hospitals (MEDIUM; The Star, 3 Sept 2023, https://the-star.co.ke/counties/nairobi/2023-09-03-why-sakaja-picked-ceos-to-run-top-county-hospitals ; check each facility's level on the Kenya Master Health Facility Registry, https://kmhfr.health.go.ke, which was unreachable today):
- County-run: Mama Lucy Kibaki Hospital (Embakasi/Umoja), Mbagathi Hospital, Pumwani Maternity Hospital, Mutuini Hospital, plus newer county hospitals and many health centres/dispensaries (county list: https://nairobi.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/Health-facilities-NCC-1.pdf, not opened).
- National (state corporations under the Ministry of Health, each with a board and a CEO): Kenyatta National Hospital (KNH); Kenyatta University Teaching, Referral and Research Hospital (KUTRRH); Mathari National Teaching and Referral Hospital; National Spinal Injury Referral Hospital. Outside Nairobi: Moi Teaching and Referral Hospital (MTRH), Eldoret. MEDIUM.
- History a Nairobi reader will remember: from 2020 to 2022 county health was run by the national Nairobi Metropolitan Services (NMS); it reverted to the county in late 2022. MEDIUM.

Accountability ladder for a Nairobi COUNTY hospital (titles HIGH/MEDIUM; NAMES deliberately omitted because they change and could not be verified today):
1. Ward/department "in-charge" (nursing officer in charge; people also say "matron").
2. Hospital head. Since Sept 2023 the four big county hospitals have a Chief Executive Officer; the former "Medical Superintendent" ("Med Sup") is now "Deputy CEO, Medical Services", and there is a Hospital Board of Management (The Star, above). Smaller hospitals still have a Medical Superintendent. Most Kenyans still say "the Med Sup".
3. County Chief Officer(s) for health (accounting officers; Nairobi splits health into more than one chief officer post).
4. County Executive Committee Member (CECM) for Health, Wellness and Nutrition (the county "health minister"). In April 2023 this was Dr Anastasia Nyalita (The Star); current holder UNVERIFIED.
5. The Governor (Johnson Sakaja, elected 2022; MEDIUM).
6. Oversight: Nairobi City County Assembly health committee (exact committee name UNVERIFIED); the Senate Standing Committee on Health (oversees counties); the National Assembly Departmental Committee on Health (chair reported as Dr James Nyikal, Dec 2025; oversees the Ministry, KNH, SHA).
For NATIONAL hospitals: hospital CEO and Board, then Principal Secretary (State Department for Medical Services), then the Cabinet Secretary for Health (Aden Duale as of Mar to Aug 2026 reporting; MEDIUM).

Terminology a Kenyan would use (MEDIUM unless sourced; confirm with a Nairobi reviewer):
- "Casualty" is the everyday word for the emergency department; official signs often say "Accident & Emergency (A&E)". Use "casualty (A&E)".

### H2. Data Protection Act, No. 24 of 2019 (HIGH; read on Kenya Law, https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2019/24/eng@2022-12-31)

- Health is sensitive data. s.2: "'sensitive personal data' means data revealing the natural person's race, health status, ethnic social origin, conscience, belief, genetic data, biometric data, property details, marital status, family details ... sex or the sexual orientation of the data subject". A report about a named or identifiable patient's treatment is sensitive personal data.
- s.44: sensitive data may be processed only if the s.25 principles apply (lawfulness, minimisation, purpose limitation, etc.); s.45 adds permitted grounds (including establishing or defending a legal claim, and protecting vital interests where the person cannot consent). s.46: health data "may only be processed— (a) by or under the responsibility of a health care provider; or (b) by a person subject to the obligation of professional secrecy under any law", which is met if processing "is necessary for reasons of public interest in the area of public health" or is done by someone who "owes a duty of confidentiality under any law". An NGO-run bot does not fit s.46 neatly: get legal advice, and keep the design that stores no names, numbers or raw messages.
- Cross-border. s.48: transfer out of Kenya only with proof to the Data Commissioner of appropriate safeguards, or where necessary for listed purposes (contract, public interest, legal claims, vital interests, compelling legitimate interests). s.49(1): "The processing of sensitive personal data out of Kenya shall only be effected upon obtaining consent of a data subject and on obtaining confirmation of appropriate safeguards." Sending report text to an AI service hosted outside Kenya is therefore a consent-and-safeguards question: the S0.privacy message should be reviewed against s.49 (it may need an explicit consent step and a statement that processing happens outside Kenya).
- s.50 lets the Cabinet Secretary require certain processing to happen on servers in Kenya; whether any such rule currently covers health data is UNVERIFIED.
- Registration: data controllers and processors must register with the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) under s.18 and the 2021 registration regulations, which make registration mandatory for health-related processing regardless of size (MEDIUM, from general knowledge; not re-read today). Data subject rights (access, correction, deletion), breach notification within 72 hours (s.43) and data protection impact assessments (s.31) also apply (MEDIUM).
- Truly anonymous aggregates (per-hospital counts) fall outside "personal data"; small cell counts at small facilities can re-identify people, so keep a minimum threshold before publishing a pattern.
