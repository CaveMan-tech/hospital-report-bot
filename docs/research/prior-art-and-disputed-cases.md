# Prior art, and viral hospital allegations that were disputed

Research for the deck's appendix slides A0 and A. Checked 21 September 2026 by opening the sources
listed; a claim is **CONFIRMED** only if a source was opened and read. **UNCONFIRMED** means it was
seen only in a search result, or the page would not open. Pages were read through a fetch tool that
summarises, so check any phrase in quotation marks against the live page before it goes on a slide.

Two sources that carry the most weight were read a second time on the live page and matched: the
Lagos Ministry of Health statement of 3 September 2026 (dates, ages, every amount, the refund dispute,
and no mention of cash demands, POS operators or network failure) and Tribune, 6 December 2023 (dead on
arrival, CCTV, no police report demanded, the criticisms, the apology).

Confidence labels: HIGH (two or more reputable sources opened, or the primary source), MEDIUM (one
reputable source opened), LOW (secondary or self-reported).

## 1. Viral allegations against hospitals that were officially disputed

Why this matters to the design: outcry moves institutions quickly, and a single account can be
wrong, incomplete or contested. That is the case for counting patterns, labelling them unverified,
and leaving verification and publication to an organisation.

Tone for all of these: nobody is said to have lied. "The panel found", "the state disputed her
account", "the records showed".

- Not "an X post": the origin reported is the bystander's viral video and YALI statements.
- Not "a threat to sue the poster": the only threat found was against hospital staff.
- Not simply "cleared": cleared of the police-report allegation, and criticised on standards and

### 1.3 General Hospital Ikot Ekpene, Akwa Ibom, 2025. MEDIUM. The cleanest example of a single account that did not hold

A woman's Facebook video alleged that staff neglected her mother in the emergency unit. The State
Hospital Management Board reviewed the records, which showed her mother was attended to from
7:45 am. The woman retracted and apologised; officials filed a police complaint.
[Premium Times, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/regional/south-south-regional/824612-woman-backtracks-apologises-after-facebook-video-alleging-hospital-neglect.html).
The police complaint is also a reminder of the legal risk to a lone poster (see
`nigeria-pack.md`, H3).

### 2.1 I Paid A Bribe. HIGH on status, MEDIUM on impact

- Launch 15 August 2010, Janaagraha: [Global Voices](https://globalvoices.org/2010/09/14/i-paid-a-bribe-an-endeavor-in-india/). Its co-founder on the aim, systemic issues rather than individuals: [Yale Insights, 2011](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/have-you-paid-bribe).
- Offline: the domain does not resolve; registry expiry 23 July 2026, status redemption period. Last capture: [Wayback, 6 Aug 2025](http://web.archive.org/web/20250806224425/https://ipaidabribe.com/).
- The Bangalore transport story, as far as sources support it: the team presented to transport officials, and the then commissioner Bhaskar Rao issued show-cause notices to 20 officers; the recommendations included replacing manual driving tests. [Governance Now, reproduced](https://daanishraza.wordpress.com/2011/08/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/using-the-web-to-fight-corruption-1.1001921). **No source opened shows the platform caused the automated test tracks.** The BBC article usually cited (6 June 2011) could not be opened.
- Critique: Tiago Peixoto, ["I Paid a Bribe. So What?"](https://democracyspot.net/2012/09/14/i-paid-a-bribe-so-what/), 2012. Ang (2014, Comparative Politics) on why the Chinese copies failed: abstract only, UNCONFIRMED.
- Other figures seen: nearly 49,000 reports across 645 towns by 2015 ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/i-paid-a-bribe-how-some-citizens-are-fighting-corruption-from-the-bottom-up-48111)), which does not sit easily with the later counter. Cite the counter as self-reported.

### 2.2 Ushahidi. HIGH

- Founded 2008 to map Kenya's post-election violence: [Ushahidi](https://www.ushahidi.com/about/our-story). About 300 reports in the first month: [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/ushahidi-the-african-software-platform-helping-victims-in-global-emergencies/). The "45,000 users" figure often repeated is UNCONFIRMED; do not use it.
- What happens to deployments: Internews / CrowdGlobe (George Washington University), ["Mapping the Maps"](https://irevolutions.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/internewswpcrowdglobe_web-1.pdf), July 2012, PDF read. 12,795 Crowdmaps in over 100 countries; 93% had fewer than 10 reports; 61% were identical to the default settings; 585 had between 21 and 10,000 reports.
- "Dead Ushahidi" (2012): [ICTworks](https://www.ictworks.org/dead-ushahidi-stark-reminder-sustainability-planning-ict4d/).
- Its own deployment and post counts differ between pages; cite only the homepage figure, with the date.

### 2.3 Care Opinion. HIGH. The closest prior art in health

- Non-profit community interest company, operating since 2005, funded by subscriptions from more than 500 organisations; Scotland funds NHS subscriptions centrally: [about](https://www.careopinion.org.uk/info/about), [Scotland](https://www.careopinion.org.uk/info/current-work-scotland).
- Every story is moderated by a person before publication so that patients and staff cannot be identified: [Locock et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8874190/), [moderation rules](https://www.careopinion.org.uk/info/moderation).
- Numbers: [site statistics](https://www.careopinion.org.uk/activitystats/activitystatsforsite). About half of stories are wholly positive.
- Evidence on whether stories lead to change:
  - Locock et al. 2020, one Scottish hospital: 77 stories, all answered, two changes recorded; staff felt anonymity made stories less credible. [PMC6978824](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6978824/)
  - Ramsey et al. 2022, three NHS trusts: one registered 215 changes; one did not respond; one sent standard replies. [PMC9580083](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9580083/)
  - Ramsey et al. 2019: five types of response, from none to "transparent, conversational". [Patient Experience Journal](https://pxjournal.org/journal/vol6/iss2/9/)
  - NIHR themed review: a quarter of nurses and over half of doctors had never changed practice because of online feedback. [NIHR](https://evidence.nihr.ac.uk/themedreview/improving-care-by-using-patient-feedback/)

## 3. "Reporting platforms decay when nobody on the other end acts"

As worded, this overstates the evidence: no source opened shows platforms decaying. What the
research does show:

1. **Most platforms draw little institutional response.** Peixoto and Fox, "When Does ICT-Enabled Citizen Voice Lead to Government Responsiveness?", IDS Bulletin 47(1), 2016. Of 23 platforms in the global South, 11 reached a medium or high response rate (20% or more) and seven were high; high uptake sat alongside low response; every high-response case had government leading or partnering. [IDS Bulletin](https://bulletin.ids.ac.uk/index.php/idsbo/article/view/34)
2. **People whose report is acted on report again.** Sjoberg, Mellon and Peixoto, "The Effect of Bureaucratic Responsiveness on Citizen Participation", Public Administration Review 77(3), 2017. Among 399,364 UK FixMyStreet users, having a first report fixed went with about a 57% higher chance of a second report (18.2% against 11.6%, modelled). An association, not a causal estimate, and about street faults. [Open copy](https://www.oidp.net/docs/repo/doc381.pdf)
3. **Technology does not close the loop alone.** McGee and others, "Appropriating technology for accountability: messages from Making All Voices Count", IDS, 2018: many funded projects failed to close the feedback loop; transparency alone is not enough; the capacities that matter are built offline. [MAVC](https://www.makingallvoicescount.org/publication/appropriating-technology-accountability-messages-making-voices-count/)
4. **In health, distrust suppresses complaints.** Chilumpha and others, Health Policy and Planning 38 (Suppl 2), 2023: Malawi has over 30 complaint mechanisms and almost nobody in the study used them, from unawareness, fear of retaliation and disbelief that anything would follow. Qualitative, Malawi. [PMC10666912](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10666912/)

Nothing specific to Nigerian health complaint systems was found.

**Wording the evidence supports.** "Reporting tools do not create responsiveness by themselves. In a
review of 23 platforms most drew little institutional response, and on FixMyStreet people whose
first report was fixed were about 57% more likely to report again."

## 4. What is honestly different about Open Ward

- **I Paid A Bribe** published every unverified report on a public site and hoped departments would react. Open Ward publishes nothing; an organisation receives only patterns of five or more reports, and it verifies and acts.
- **Ushahidi** is software anyone can deploy, usually with nobody owning the response, which is why most maps stayed empty. Open Ward is one deployment with a fixed recipient and a fixed use for the data.
- **Care Opinion** publishes individual stories and depends on providers subscribing and replying. Open Ward publishes no individual story and assumes no cooperation from the hospital.
- **Tracka** follows visible projects with named field staff, and BudgIT publishes. Open Ward collects anonymous patient experience behind a threshold and leaves publishing to the partner.
- **SERVICOM** is an internal, identified complaint channel inside the federal government. Open Ward is external, anonymous, and reports patterns to an independent advocate.
- What Open Ward adds that none of them do: help for the reporter first (their right, what to do now), whatever becomes of the report.
